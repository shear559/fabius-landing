FABIUS / DEMONSTRATIONS / SEPTEMBER 30, 2026

Three working systems.
Every transition inspectable.

These products illustrate work made with Fabius. They are deterministic local demonstrations, not a controlled measurement of the plugin’s effect.

How they were made

Three independent Codex build sessions received separate briefs, separate output folders and the Fabius 3.3.0 operating rules. Each loaded the lean core, engineering process and design guidance; the security and agent-team builds also loaded their domain guidance. A coordinating session integrated the products, tested their visible behavior in a browser and refined the previews and guided tours.

An initial attempt to use the previous Claude-based build procedure stopped at the account’s session limit before producing a build. These are the subsequent Codex builds. They are not paired runs, and their results do not establish a model-to-model comparison.

Checks that actually ran

  • Sentinel: 20 fixed policy cases, 27 malformed-input cases and all 768 valid context/action combinations. The evaluator is deterministic, does not mutate input and denies unknown or unreadable context.
  • Relay: 21,977 checked state transitions. The checks exercise worker interruption, restore, replay in every phase, immutable inputs, queue ownership and output uniqueness.
  • Cohort: 12 scheduler checks, including both specialist completion orders, concurrency limits, failure/retry, complete artifact assembly and a mutation control that proves the cap assertion catches a removed limit.
  • Browser integration: Chromium and WebKit at 390 and 1440 CSS pixels, including the production-style sandbox and content policy, working controls, guided tours, all nine gallery tabs, dialog dismissal and keyboard focus. Results are published below.

Counts describe these specific assertions and fixtures. They are not quality scores, performance benchmarks or evidence that a production system is secure. Automated WebKit ran on an Intel Mac; no physical phone was tested.
